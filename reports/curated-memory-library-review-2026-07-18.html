Make remembering feel effortless.

Our Little World has real curation, media, voice, and family-sharing foundations. The next step is to stop presenting them as separate tools and turn them into one calm nightly ritual: a few of the right memories, already sorted, ready for a parent to keep and color in.

Executive decision

The product direction is right. The experience is not assembled yet.

The codebase contains most of the hard primitives. What it lacks is the durable system and focused flow that can turn a parent's entire library into a small, ongoing memory ritual.

“Each day, we quietly find a few memories worth keeping. You confirm what belongs; your family record organizes itself.”

Keep the product centered on

  • Today: tonight's few best memories
  • Add: intentional capture
  • Our World: the durable family record
  • Print: optional output, never the habit
P0 finding: historical scan candidates are not a durable backlog. The scan controller keeps candidates in memory, while a completed scan advances its checkpoint. Later scans normally look back only three days. A terminated app can therefore lose unreviewed historical candidates and fail to pace them back out over future evenings. The first build must add a private on-device candidate ledger and separate “analyzed” from “reviewed.”
Where we are

Strong plumbing, incomplete parent journey.

The foundation is materially better than the screenshots suggest. The child-identity admission bug seen in physical build 1.1.6 was corrected in 1.1.7. The next risk is no longer only matching accuracy; it is whether the right candidates can survive, pace, and resurface.

What must change

Six product gaps are creating most of the friction.

1

Persist the backlog

Store private candidates, clusters, reasons, queue state, and decisions in local SQLite. Scanned cannot mean reviewed.

2

Turn Today into one ritual

Today currently mixes nudges, prompts, digest, Tonight links, milestone, timeline, months, Places, and On This Day. Make one queue the clear primary action.

3

Replace photo administration

The dense review grid is a useful advanced utility. The normal experience should be one media card at a time with the best decision already made.

4

Put enrichment in the flow

Text, voice, emoji, tags, Firsts, and replies exist, but are fragmented across forms and detail screens. Bring them into the review pager.

5

Actually organize for parents

The current “scene” layer uses time, location, and written keywords—not visual understanding. Add automatic collections with clear evidence and easy correction.

6

Ground developmental context

Connect dates to parent-confirmed Firsts and events. Never turn an image guess or an age norm into family history.

Target experience

Five calm cards, not five different tools.

The nightly flow

At the chosen time, notify only when worthwhile media is ready. Open directly into three to seven photo or video cards. The app explains why each one matters; the parent confirms, speaks, types, reacts, or simply advances.

  1. Recent and historical picks are mixed.
  2. Strong defaults arrive preselected after trust is earned.
  3. Text, voice, and categories auto-save as drafts.
  4. The native picker is always one tap away.
  5. The final card says “That's tonight.” No streaks or guilt.

The first-year shape

Use “top 500” as a target, not a hard limit.

Recommended first-year target

  • Up to 365 daily anchors where eligible media exists
  • Roughly 100–200 distinct standouts and special videos
  • More on exceptional days; honest gaps on empty days
  • No weak media added merely to hit a number

Pace that fits real life

Five historical candidates per active evening processes about 500 candidates in 100 sessions. “Keep going” can accelerate catch-up. After identity trust is earned and auto-save is explicitly enabled, the app can keep clear anchors automatically and use Tonight mainly for correction and context.

Production partner model

One coherent family album without mirroring either camera roll.

Discovery is private to each phone. Memories become shared when a parent keeps them.

From your phone

Only that parent sees unsaved candidates, local asset IDs, face data, fingerprints, skips, and the private backlog.

In your world

Both parents see kept memories and can add separate text, voice, emoji, collection changes, and confirmed Firsts.

If both parents own the same photo

After saving, group likely duplicate uploads as one event presentation. Preserve both parents' originals and authored context; do not silently delete or destructively merge.

If only one parent has a photo

The other parent never sees the unsaved candidate. Once kept, it becomes an ordinary shared family moment that either writer can enrich.

Architecture

Keep private analysis local; share only the memory.

New on-device state

  • discovery_candidates: media facts, quality, day, reason, state
  • candidate_clusters: burst/event representative and members
  • review_queue_items: nightly rank, draft, commit and resume state
  • candidate_feedback: loop-specific keep/skip/category corrections

The scan checkpoint records analysis progress. Review state is separate and cannot be erased by a successful scan.

Shared family state

  • Reuse moments, media, voice notes, reactions, Firsts, Letters, views, and replies
  • Add automatic and parent-created collections
  • Add separately authored text/voice annotations
  • Add source-linked factual context where useful
  • Add privacy-safe post-save event grouping

Unsaved media should not be sent to a remote AI service. Optional server enrichment begins only after a parent keeps the memory.

Context rule: “Twelve days after the First ‘rolled over’ that you saved” is allowed because it derives from two confirmed dates. “They had just learned to roll” is not allowed because it converts an interpretation into history.
Delivery sequence

Build the reliable memory loop before the smarter layers.

Release 0

Trustworthy backlog + product cut

  • Persistent candidates, clusters, queue, and decisions
  • Separate analyzed and reviewed state
  • Crash-safe resume and 5,000-item fixture
  • Simpler Today; remove misaligned reveal/book nudges
Done when: an app termination cannot lose historical candidates or make them unreachable.
Release 1

Tonight's memories MVP

  • Full-screen photo/video pager
  • 3–7 recent + historical cards
  • Keep, skip, choose another, favorite, text, voice
  • Queue-ready notification and end card
Done when: a parent finishes five cards without visiting Add, Review, or Moment Detail.
Release 2

First-year catch-up engine

  • Coverage-aware selection
  • Distinct events and special videos
  • Adaptive pace and Keep going
  • iCloud, limited access, battery and deletion hardening
Done when: a 5,000-item family grows its first-year record over repeated sessions without repeat or full rescan.
Release 3

Automatic collections

  • Factual and behavior-based collections
  • Validated on-device visual suggestions
  • Selected-by-default category chips
  • Correction learning isolated from identity
Done when: most kept media requires zero filing actions and is still easy to find.
Release 4

Grounded context + shared enrichment

  • Confirmed-First adjacency
  • Separate partner annotations
  • Shared saved-memory lookbacks
  • Cross-parent duplicate event grouping
Done when: every developmental statement has a source and neither parent can see the other's unsaved library.

Decisions to hold

What not to build into the core loop

  • No wholesale camera-roll mirror
  • No manual chapter filing requirement
  • No fixed top-500 deletion cap
  • No image-inferred Firsts
  • No print or book-readiness daily nudge
  • No default 20-item nightly feed
  • No uncertain identity in the default keep lane
  • No category feedback contaminating identity matching
Immediate build slice

Prove the loop with the smallest complete release.

  1. Persist candidates and separate analyzed/reviewed state.
  2. Generate a deterministic recent + historical queue.
  3. Ship `/tonight` with keep, skip, and one-line text.
  4. Resume safely after crash or relaunch.
  5. Simplify Today and fix the Tonight notification link.
  6. Validate with a 5,000-item fixture and real physical library.

Then layer in

Voice, automatic categories, partner annotations, and milestone context should follow on the same architecture. They should not delay proving that the app can reliably find, retain, pace, and resurface the right memories.
